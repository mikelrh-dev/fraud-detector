Chapter 05

Ensemble and Dynamic Thresholds

Risk policy as configuration: dynamic thresholds and the human review band.

A fixed cutoff would be wrong: blocking every $50 transaction above score 45 would drown analysts in false positives, while waiting for score 70 on a $60,000 transfer would be negligent. The decision threshold therefore scales inversely with amount:

Amount TierFraud Threshold
$0 – $1,000≥ 70
$1,001 – $10,000≥ 50
$10,001 – $50,000≥ 45
$50,001+≥ 40

The intuition: the more money moves, the less certainty you demand before acting. Scores between 75% of the tier threshold and the threshold itself land in a review band — flagged for human analysis rather than auto-blocked. This band is where the human-in-the-loop design lives: the system narrows thousands of transactions down to a reviewable queue, and analysts make the final call.

Why not let ML decide alone?

The ensemble deliberately gives rules the largest weight (0.60). When an analyst asks "why was this blocked?", the answer often starts with a named, weighted rule — auditable logic — reinforced by model evidence. Pure-ML blocks are harder to defend and impossible to tune surgically.

What happens when a layer goes silent

The three weights sum to 1.00, which creates a quiet failure mode. If the ML model is not loaded, MLModelService.predict returns 0.0 — and 0.0 is indistinguishable from "the model ran and found nothing". The obvious implementation multiplies that zero by its 0.25 weight and averages the rest, which means every single score silently loses 25 points, with no log line and no degraded status. The worst part is the timing: the loss is largest exactly when the system is already in trouble.

So the layers were given a way to say "I have nothing to say" that is distinct from "I ran and the answer is zero". A layer now returns None, and the ensemble redistributes that weight across the layers that did produce a value, normalised to sum to 1.0 — so a surviving layer carries the full weight rather than a shrunken share. A layer that genuinely scored 0.0 keeps its weight, because that is real evidence of low risk.

Two consequences make the degraded state visible instead of hypothetical. A degraded_ml_layer counter increments on every such transaction and is served in the counters block of GET /health/workers — a signal of its own, separate from the per-stream status beside it, which can only ever be ok, backed_up or error. That separation is the point: consumer-group pending depth cannot express this failure, because the code path in question drains that very queue. And a non-finite score from any layer fails closed to 100.0 rather than open: min/max propagate NaN unchanged, every NaN > threshold comparison is False, and the naive path would quietly classify the transaction as legitimate.
